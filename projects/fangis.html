The Problem

In today's world most activities are done on a screen. Let it be entertainment, education or games. With fangis we wanted to create a game that gets people outside, moving and interacting with the real world. The classic game of tag was joined with challenges and a digital spin to create a new experience.

The Process

As lead designer, I was responsible for the overall appearance of the app, as well as the user experience and interaction design. Together with the game designer and backend developer we defined game flows, user flows and the overall structure of the app. The design process was iterative and involved multple rounds of feedback. The final design was implemented in Nuxt.js and Vue.js

The Solution

The final UI keeps the map secondary to the action — big, glanceable status cards for "catcher" and "runner" roles, a simple distance readout instead of a full map by default, and a single primary action per screen.

Reflection & Takeaways

Being both designer and frontend dev on this one meant design decisions got reality-checked fast — some "clever" interactions got simplified once they were actually built. Next time I'd prototype the GPS-distance interaction earlier, since it ended up shaping the whole layout.
